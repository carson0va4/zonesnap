Workspaces

A workspace is a setup you switch on in one go: a layout, apps to open and snap, and options. Gaming mode is the Gaming workspace. Only one is on at a time: Default is on whenever no other workspace is. Click a workspace to open or close its settings. Changes here save straight away.

When switching between workspaces
What happens to apps the previous workspace opened. Games, and apps the new workspace also uses, are never touched.

Apps

One list of apps for every workspace: each workspace opens the apps it uses and snaps them into their zones. To add an app, put its window in a zone and press Ctrl+Alt+A (with a workspace on, it's placed in that workspace too). Changes here save straight away.

Where each app goes
Drag an app onto a zone to move it there

Where each app goes when you come back to Default, after gaming mode or another workspace switches off. Leave where it is keeps it where that workspace left it; Put back returns it to where it was before that workspace switched on.

Game layouts

Games start auto gaming mode and are made borderless while it's on. Layout: a game can have its own layout, such as 4:3 for a retro game, used instead of the Gaming layout while it's running; your side apps go to the zones covering their usual spots. Choose Its own layout, then Edit. Set the game itself to windowed mode at the game zone's size.

Hotkeys

Click Change, then press the keys you want. Esc cancels; Backspace removes the shortcut.
